

# **The Student Flourishing Blueprint: An Evidence-Based Framework for a Digital Mental Health Intervention**

## **Part 1: The Student Mental Health Crisis: A Market Defined by Nuance**

### **1.1 The Statistical Landscape: An Epidemic in Decline?**

The narrative surrounding student mental health is one of staggering, persistent need. Recent data from 2024 and 2025 indicates that while the unprecedented peaks of the post-pandemic era may be receding, the prevalence of mental distress remains exceptionally high.

Key 2025 findings show a campus population still struggling:

* **Depression:** 37% of students report moderate-to-severe depressive symptoms, a decrease from 44% in 2022\. Severe depression fell from 23% to 18%.1  
* **Anxiety:** 32% of students report moderate-to-severe anxiety symptoms, down from 37% in 2022\.1 In 2024, anxiety symptoms were reported by 34% of students 3, and 36% reported a formal diagnosis.4  
* **Suicidal Ideation:** Students who seriously considered suicide in the past year dropped from 15% in 2022 to 11% in 2025\.1  
* **Loneliness:** 52% of students report high levels of loneliness, a reduction from 58% in 2022\.1 Other 2023 data places this figure at 53%.4

While these downward trends are positive, they must be contextualized. A 37% prevalence rate for moderate-to-severe depression indicates that more than one-third of the student population meets the criteria for a significant clinical issue. Furthermore, other persistent conditions remain concerning, with 13% of students reporting eating disorders and 5% substance abuse disorder in 2024\.5

### **1.2 The Paradox of Flourishing: Beyond Sickness to "Languishing"**

The most critical data point for any product strategy is not the decline in depression, but the corresponding data on well-being. While fewer students report *struggling* with depression and anxiety, they are not necessarily experiencing *higher* levels of well-being.2

Reported levels of "flourishing"—a psychological state of high well-being defined by self-esteem, purpose, and optimism—dropped to 36% in 2025 after reaching 38% in 2024\.2 This reveals a critical nuance: the simple absence of depression is not the same as the presence of mental wellness.

This creates a "Paradox of Flourishing." The data suggests a large and growing cohort of students who are not clinically "sick" but are decidedly "not well." They are *languishing*. This population—the 64% who are *not* flourishing 2—represents the single largest, most underserved market in student mental health. They are unlikely to seek formal therapy but are in desperate need of tools to build resilience, purpose, and connection.

### **1.3 The Primary Stressors: Drivers of Student Distress**

To build an effective tool, one must treat the disease, not just the symptoms. The primary drivers of this crisis are multifaceted, stemming from academic, social, and financial pressures.

* **Academic and General Stress:** This is the most-cited concern. In a 2024 report, 65% of student clients identified both academic performance and stress as top concerns, followed by anxiety (47%) and depression (46%).3 The pressure to perform, manage high workloads, and avoid procrastination creates a significant source of burnout.6 Mental health struggles, in turn, negatively impact academic performance for 83% of students.8  
* **Social Isolation and Loneliness:** As a generation, 79% of adults aged 18-24 report feeling lonely, a rate far higher than any other age group.6 This is exacerbated by the college transition—a major life upheaval involving new social landscapes and living away from home.6 Students who feel lonely are over four times more likely to experience psychological distress.6  
* **Social Media as an Accelerant:** The digital social landscape often worsens these feelings. Social media is directly linked to negative mental health effects, including "FOMO" (Fear of Missing Out), negative social comparison, and a "filtered reality" that contributes to inadequacy and depression.10 It is also correlated with sleep disruptions, cyberbullying, and increased anxiety.10

Any successful intervention must therefore target not only the *symptoms* (anxiety, depression) but also the *drivers* (academic burnout, loneliness).

## **Part 2: The Clinical Toolkit: Evidence-Based Interventions**

### **2.1 The "Dual-Track" Strategy: A Model for a Fragmented Market**

The "Paradox of Flourishing" 2 demands a "Dual-Track" product strategy. A single-focus app (e.g., only mindfulness, or only therapy) will fail to capture the total market.

* **Track 1: The Reactive Toolkit (For the *Floundering*):** This track is for students experiencing acute distress. It provides immediate, in-the-moment relief for panic, severe anxiety, and depressive spirals. The goal is symptom reduction and crisis mitigation.  
* **Track 2: The Proactive Toolkit (For the *Languishing*):** This track is for the non-flourishing majority. It provides tools to build long-term skills, resilience, and well-being. The goal is not just to get *well*, but to *stay well* and ultimately *flourish*.

### **2.2 Track 1 Interventions: Tools for Acute Distress (Floundering)**

**Cognitive Behavioral Therapy (CBT):** CBT is the most robustly validated intervention for anxiety and depression.13 A self-guided CBT-based app can significantly reduce anxiety in young adults.15 A comprehensive digital CBT toolkit should include six evidence-based techniques 16:

1. Psychoeducation  
2. Behavioral Activation (activity scheduling)  
3. Cognitive Restructuring (thought records)  
4. Problem Solving  
5. Relaxation Techniques  
6. Exposure Techniques (for anxiety)

**Grounding Techniques:** For acute panic attacks, grounding techniques are the first line of defense.17 They work by interrupting the 'fight, flight, or freeze' response and activating the parasympathetic nervous system.18 This is achieved by shifting focus from distressing thoughts to concrete, sensory input.17 A key, easily teachable technique is the **5-4-3-2-1 method** 20, which involves identifying:

* 5 things you can see  
* 4 things you can touch  
* 3 things you can hear  
* 2 things you can smell  
* 1 thing you can taste

### **2.3 Track 2 Interventions: Tools for Building Well-Being (Languishing)**

**Positive Psychology & Flourishing:** To move students from languishing to flourishing, interventions from positive psychology are essential.21 These include:

* **Mindfulness:** Teaches non-judgmental awareness of one's emotional state.22 Simple exercises include body scans, mindful breathing, and paying attention to sensory details.23  
* **Gratitude Journaling:** A proven method to boost happiness, life satisfaction, and resilience.25  
* **Strengths Identification:** Helping students identify and use their character strengths.21

**Academic Burnout & Time Management:** Since academic pressure is the \#1 stressor 3, time management is a clinical imperative for preventing burnout.27 A Strategic Time Management Programme (STMP) has been shown to reduce burnout, anxiety, and depression in university students.28 Key strategies include 7:

* **Setting SMART Goals:** Specific, Meaningful, Achievable, Relevant, and Time-bound.  
* **Task Prioritization:** Using a to-do list and prioritizing tasks. A key tip is "Do most important tasks first thing in the morning."  
* **Avoiding Procrastination & Multitasking:** Focusing on one task at a time.  
* **Scheduling Breaks:** Intentionally scheduling time for rest, social activities, and enjoyable hobbies.

### **2.4 The Loneliness Solution: Peer and Social Support**

This is the most critical intervention for the social-emotional component of the crisis. A 2022 systematic review on interventions for student loneliness provided a clear and powerful directive 31:

* **80% Effective:** Interventions based on **social support groups, increasing social interaction, or reflective exercises**.  
* **50% Effective:** Interventions based on **psychoeducation** (e.g., just reading articles or watching videos about loneliness).

The conclusion is inescapable: a digital intervention for loneliness *must be interactive*. It must facilitate *actual connection* or *proactively prompt* social behaviors.

This is why the **Peer-to-Peer Support Model** is so effective.32 Students are more likely to seek help from friends than from a mental health provider.32 Peer support models, like those used by 'TalkCampus' or 'Active Minds', create a scalable, stigma-free, 24/7 environment where students can share experiences that may not require full clinical intervention.32 They provide a sense of community and belonging, directly counteracting the loneliness epidemic.33

## **Part 3: Digital Translation: A Blueprint for the Student Mental Health App**

The following framework translates the clinical interventions from Part 2 into a concrete, actionable product design. The app's architecture will be built on the "Dual-Track" strategy.

The central design specification is summarized in Table 1\.

### **3.1 Core Therapeutic Modules (The "CBT Toolkit")**

This module forms the foundation of Track 1 (Reactive) and provides skills for Track 2 (Proactive). It will digitize the core CBT techniques.16

* **Feature 1: "Thought Reframer" (Cognitive Restructuring Diary):** This is the app's cornerstone CBT tool. It's a guided journal based on the "Cognitive Diary" 35 and "Thought Diary" 14 models. When a user feels distressed, they are guided through a step-by-step evaluation 35:  
  1. **Situation:** What happened?  
  2. **Emotion:** What did you feel? (Rate 1-10)  
  3. **Automatic Thought:** What was the "hot" thought (e.g., "I'm a failure")?  
  4. **Challenge:** The app presents prompts to challenge "Warped Thoughts".14 (e.g., "What is the evidence *against* this thought?").  
  5. **Balanced Thought:** What is a more realistic, balanced perspective?  
  6. **Re-rate:** Re-rate the initial emotion.  
* **Feature 2: "Fear Ladder" (Exposure Technique):** This tool targets anxiety and phobias, particularly social anxiety. It is based on the "ladder function" 36 found in apps like 'MindShift CBT'.37 The user defines a fear (e.g., "Speaking in class"). The app then helps them build a 5-10 rung "ladder" of small, achievable steps (e.g., Rung 1: "Think about asking a question"; Rung 5: "Ask a question in a small discussion"; Rung 10: "Give a presentation"). The app tracks their progress as they complete each rung.  
* **Feature 3: "Belief Experiment" (Behavioral Activation):** This feature is a powerful tool for social anxiety.40 It guides the user to treat their negative beliefs as testable hypotheses.  
  1. **Identify Belief:** "If I share my ideas, people will think I'm stupid".39  
  2. **Make Prediction:** "How anxious will I feel (0-100)?", "What is the % chance my fear will come true?"  
  3. **Run Experiment:** The app encourages a small, safe test (e.g., "Share one idea in a breakout room").  
  4. **Record Outcome:** "What actually happened?", "How anxious did I *really* feel?", "Did people think I was stupid?"  
  5. **Reflect:** "What does this outcome say about my original belief?"

### **3.2 The "In-Case-of-Emergency" Module (The Reactive Feature)**

This module is the core of Track 1, designed for acute panic. It must be accessible from the app's home screen as a single, large button. The design is modeled on the "Rootr" feature from the 'Rootd' app.34

This "Anchor" button, when pressed, provides an immediate, simple choice 34:

1. **"I need a Distraction."** (Mental Grounding)  
2. **"I need a Technique."** (Physical/Sensory Grounding)

If the user chooses **"Distraction,"** the app launches a simple mental grounding game. This is *not* a "fun" game but a clinical tool. Examples include "Name 5 states that start with 'M'" or "Categorize these items".19 This forces cognitive effort away from the source of panic.

If the user chooses **"Technique,"** the app launches a guided audio exercise for the **5-4-3-2-1 Technique**.20 A calm voice walks the user through the process ("First, look around you. Find five things you can see..."). This engages the senses, activates the parasympathetic nervous system, and "anchors" the user to the present moment, breaking the 'fight-or-flight' feedback loop.18

### **3.3 The Social Connection Hub (The Proactive Feature)**

This module is the core of Track 2 and directly addresses the 80% efficacy rate for interactive loneliness interventions.31

* **Feature 1: "The Nod" (Proactive Social Challenges):** This feature is based on the 'Nod' app, which was designed specifically for student loneliness.42 The app proactively delivers "social challenges"—small, concrete activities to build connections.42 Examples: "Send a 'thank you' text to someone from a class," or "Ask a classmate what they thought of the lecture." Crucially, this is paired with a **"Reflection"** feature, which provides a brief cognitive reframing exercise to normalize the anxiety of reaching out.42  
* **Feature 2: "The Quad" (Moderated Peer Support Forum):** This is the 24/7, anonymous "social support group".33 Its success is 100% dependent on its **safety and moderation** infrastructure. An unmoderated forum will become toxic and harmful. The moderation must be a hybrid model:  
  1. **AI/Machine Learning:** Use advanced, real-time AI to classify and flag high-risk posts (e.g., suicide, self-harm, bullying) in milliseconds, mirroring the 'TalkCampus' model.43  
  2. **Professional Administrators:** A 24/7 paid clinical staff that reviews all high-risk flagged content, escalates users to crisis helplines, and has the power to ban users.44  
  3. **Trained Volunteer "Listeners":** Community members who are trained to remove low-level inappropriate content (which is then reviewed by Admins) and offer supportive, non-clinical responses.44  
  4. **User-Side Tools:** Give users full control to block, flag, and hide content, as well as set "personalized trigger warnings" for their feed.52

### **3.4 Passive and Active Data Collection (Trackers and Journals)**

* **Feature 1: "Mood Tracker":** This is a standard feature 55 that allows users to log moods and identify patterns and triggers.56 However, analysis shows users often *avoid* logging negative moods.59 A strategic design must solve this:  
  * **Solution 1:** Make logging negative moods *productive*. When a user logs "Sad," the app should immediately respond: "Sorry to hear that. Would you like to try a 2-minute 'Thought Reframer' or a 'Mindful Breathing' exercise?" This connects the *log* (problem) to a *tool* (solution).  
  * **Solution 2:** Provide *interpretive* feedback. Users want recommendations based on their data.59 The app should provide visualizations (graphs, calendars) and simple interpretations: "We notice your mood tends to dip on Sunday evenings. This is common. Here is a 'Proactive Planner' tool to help you organize your week and reduce stress."  
* **Feature 2: "Journal and Prompt Library":** This is a private, encrypted space for free-writing.60 It will be paired with a searchable library of guided journaling prompts, sourced from clinical research, that users can filter by goal:  
  * **"Ease Anxiety"** 61  
  * **"Manage Depression"** 63  
  * **"Build Gratitude"** 25  
  * **"Self-Discovery"** 62

## **Part 4: Market Landscape and Competitive Analysis**

The proposed app does not enter an empty market. It enters as a strategic hybrid, combining the best-in-class features from four distinct market models.

**Table 2: Competitive Analysis of Leading Mental Health App Models**

| App(s) | Primary Therapeutic Model | Target Audience | Key Differentiator | Pricing Model |
| :---- | :---- | :---- | :---- | :---- |
| **Calm** / **Headspace** | Mindfulness, Meditation, Sleep, Content Library 66 | General Population | Vast library of high-production-value audio/visual content for self-care.68 | Freemium (Subscription) |
| **Wysa** | AI-CBT, Empathetic Chatbot 70 | General (B2B/B2C) | AI-driven "therapeutic alliance" 72; 24/7 conversational support; scalable.72 | Freemium (B2B, B2C) |
| **BetterHelp** / **Talkspace** | Telehealth (Service Delivery) 73 | General (Adults, Teens) | Connects users with a licensed human therapist for text, video, or phone sessions.75 | Subscription (Service) |
| **Rootd** | Niche (Panic Attacks) | Acute Anxiety Sufferers | Vertically-focused "Rootr" panic button 34; education \+ short/long-term lessons.34 | Freemium (Subscription) |
| **TalkCampus** | Niche (Peer Support) | Students Only | 24/7, anonymous, student-only peer support 33; world-class safety and moderation.43 | B2B (University Partnerships) |

### **4.1 The Self-Care Library Model (Calm and Headspace)**

These are the market's "super-apps," built on a content-first model of mindfulness, meditation, and sleep stories.66 The key strategic development, however, is their move *upmarket* into clinical care. 'Calm Health', the B2B arm, now offers personalized, psychologist-written programs based on **Cognitive Behavioral Therapy (CBT), Acceptance and Commitment Therapy (ACT), and Dialectical Behavioral Therapy (DBT)**.79

This move validates the "Dual-Track" strategy. The market leader has recognized that mindfulness *alone* is insufficient and is integrating the exact clinical tools (CBT, DBT) needed for a comprehensive solution.

### **4.2 The AI Companion Model (Wysa)**

Wysa is an AI chatbot that provides "empathetic" conversation while delivering evidence-based skills.70 Its strategic importance lies in its proven ability to build a "therapeutic alliance"—the bond of trust between a client and therapist. Research shows that Wysa's AI builds a "bond subscale score" that is **comparable to human-delivered, face-to-face psychotherapy**.72 Users personify the bot and express gratitude towards it. This finding is transformative: it de-risks the concept of a non-human companion and proves that a scalable, low-cost solution *can* be perceived as supportive and effective.

### **4.3 The Service Delivery Model (BetterHelp and Talkspace)**

These platforms are not self-help apps; they are telehealth marketplaces that match users with licensed therapists.73 'Talkspace' also offers psychiatry and medication management.74 These high-cost, high-touch services are *complements*, not direct *competitors*. The proposed app is the "90% solution" for the 90% of students who are languishing or need immediate, low-level tools. It can act as a "feeder system," identifying users who require a higher level of care and providing a warm handoff to on-campus counseling 85 or telehealth providers.

### **4.4 The Niche and Specialized Apps (Rootd and TalkCampus)**

These apps succeed by *not* trying to be 'Calm'.

* **Rootd** focuses vertically on a single, high-acuity *problem*: panic attacks. Its "Rootr" button is a best-in-class example of a specialized, reactive tool.34  
* **TalkCampus** focuses vertically on a single *audience*: students. It provides one service—peer support—and does it exceptionally well, with a primary focus on safety and moderation.33

These apps prove that a focused, student-centric (TalkCampus) or problem-centric (Rootd) strategy is highly viable.

## **Part 5: The Engagement Engine: Behavioral Psychology and Ethical Gamification**

An app only works if it is used. The user's query about the "Duolingo bird" is a question about engagement, which is driven by behavioral psychology.

### **5.1 Case Study: The Duolingo "Hook Loop" Model**

High-retention apps like Duolingo are masters of the "Hook Loop Model": Trigger, Action, Reward, Investment.86

* **Trigger:** Personalized, mascot-driven push notifications from "Duo the owl".87  
* **Action:** A "ridiculously easy" one-lesson-a-day.88  
* **Variable Reward:** XP points, badges, leaderboard progression.87  
* **Investment:** The **Streak**.

The Streak is Duolingo's most powerful mechanic.90 It is built on the psychological principle of **Loss Aversion**.87 The longer the streak, the more value the user has invested, and the more psychologically painful it is to break the chain and reset to zero.93 The "Streak Freeze" is the *monetization* of this loss aversion—it allows a user to pay to relieve the anxiety of breaking their chain.89

### **5.2 The Virtual Pet: A "Tamagotchi" for Mental Wellness**

The user's "bird" idea is a "virtual pet" mechanic, a powerful tool for building emotional investment and companionship.95 Caring for a virtual pet can teach empathy and reduce loneliness.98 Even just viewing virtual animal stimuli can increase well-being.99

However, this mechanic carries **critical ethical risks** in a mental health context:

1. **Shallow Connection:** It is *not* a real pet. It cannot provide the "depth of emotional connection" or "long-term emotional support" of a real animal.98  
2. **Unrealistic Expectations:** Users may become frustrated when the pet "fails to meet those expectations".98  
3. **Negative Reinforcement:** This is the most dangerous risk. A pet that *must* be "cared for" becomes a *burden*. For a student with depression, "failing" to care for their virtual pet *reinforces* their feelings of failure. It creates *more* stress, not less. It could also reinforce negative behaviors, such as a user expressing "suppressed anger on virtual pets without knowing any real-world consequences".98

Strategic Recommendation: The "Companion" Model:  
The mechanic must be inverted. The pet should not be a burden to be cared for, but a mirror of the user's self-care.

* **Design:** The app features a simple, non-judgmental "Companion" (a plant, animal, or abstract shape).  
* **Mechanic:** The Companion *flourishes* when the user engages in self-care (e.g., completes a "Thought Reframer," does a 5-minute meditation). It *never* gets sick, dies, or looks sad. If the user is inactive, the Companion simply waits, a calm, stable presence.  
* **Result:** This design captures the *investment* and *companion* benefits 97 while completely eliminating the *ethical risks* of pressure, shame, and negative reinforcement.98

### **5.3 Ethical Gamification vs. Digital Addiction**

Gamification in a health app must be fundamentally different from a game. The central ethical guideline is that gamification must **"support the delivery of an intervention's 'active ingredient'"** (i.e., the CBT) and *not* "overshadow or distort user motivations".100

The "dark patterns" of gaming—such as public leaderboards that foster negative social comparison 87, or addictive loops that can become a behavioral addiction 101—are counter-therapeutic.

The Solution: The "Shame-Free Streak"  
The Duolingo streak is effective but punishing and shame-based.93 A mental health app must use a supportive streak.

1. **"Streak Pause" / "Weekend Mode":** Allow users to proactively pause their streak ("It's exam week," "I'm on vacation") without penalty.103  
2. **"Forgiving" Streaks:** Focus on *momentum*, not *perfection*.105 Instead of a single number that resets to zero, show a 30-day calendar grid. Missing one day leaves one empty box. The goal is to "fill the grid," not "never miss a day."  
3. **No "Streak Freeze":** The app will *not* monetize loss aversion.91 Pauses are free. This is non-negotiable for an ethical product. It builds trust by aligning the app's goals (well-being) with the user's, rather than with monetization.

**Table 3: Adapting Gamification Mechanics for Ethical Mental Health Engagement**

| Engagement Mechanic | "Dark Pattern" (Gaming / Duolingo) | "Ethical Adaptation" (Student Wellness App) | Rationale / Guiding Principles |
| :---- | :---- | :---- | :---- |
| **Streaks** | Punishing; resets to zero on missed day, creates anxiety. | **"Shame-Free Streak"**; focuses on momentum. Allows free "pauses" for exams/weekends. | .103 Avoids punishing users for being busy or unwell. |
| **Rewards** | Extrinsic (XP, points, gems) to create a "slot machine" effect.87 | **Intrinsic**; a "Companion" pet flourishes, or a progress bar for a *real* goal (e.g., "social anxiety"). | .98 The reward is the user's own well-being, not a digital token. |
| **Social** | Public leaderboards that drive social comparison and status-seeking.89 | **Private, Moderated Peer Support**. "Kudos" or "support" buttons, not competition. | .33 Fosters community and belonging, not comparison and anxiety. |
| **Loss Aversion** | **"Streak Freeze"** 91; monetizes the user's fear of losing their progress. | **None.** The "forgiving streak" has no loss to avert. Pauses are free and encouraged. | .100 Gamification must *support* the well-being goal, not *distort* it for profit. |

### **5.4 Recommendation: A 4-Pillar Engagement Strategy**

The app's engagement strategy will be built on genuine value, not addictive loops.

1. **Purpose:** The app's goals are 100% aligned with the user's health goals.106  
2. **Progress:** Show *meaningful* progress. Not "You earned 50 XP," but "You've successfully reframed 10 negative thoughts this week," or "Your average anxiety rating for 'social events' has dropped 20%."  
3. **Personalization:** The app is adaptive. It learns which tools work for the user (e.g., "You seem to like 'Mindful Walking'. Want to try that?").  
4. **Privacy:** All data, especially journal entries and mood logs, is private, encrypted, and never shared.107 This is the foundation of the trust required for a mental health tool.

## **Part 6: Strategic Synthesis and Final Recommendations**

### **6.1 The Market Opportunity**

The student mental health crisis is defined by the "Paradox of Flourishing".2 The largest unserved market is not the clinically ill, but the *languishing*: students who are not sick, but not well. The opportunity is to build a *proactive well-being tool* (Track 2\) that *also* includes a best-in-class *reactive crisis toolkit* (Track 1).

### **6.2 The "Dual-Track" Product Strategy**

The final product must serve both markets simultaneously:

* **Track 1 (Reactive):** The "In-Case-of-Emergency" toolkit. This features the "Anchor" panic button (modeled on 'Rootd') 20, guided mindfulness 23, and the full CBT Toolkit for acute anxiety/depression.16  
* **Track 2 (Proactive):** The "Flourishing Engine." This features the "Social Connection Hub" (modeled on 'Nod' and 'TalkCampus') 31, the "Journal and Prompt Library" 26, and the "Proactive Planner" for time management.30

### **6.3 The "Sticky" and Ethical Engagement Model**

The app's retention will be driven not by *punishing* users (like the Duolingo streak) but by *supporting* them. "Dark Patterns" will be replaced with "Ethical Engagement":

* **Instead of** a punishing, anxiety-inducing streak, the app will use a **"Shame-Free Streak"** with free pauses.103  
* **Instead of** a "pet" that creates a burden of care, the app will use a **"Companion"** that non-judgmentally *reflects* the user's self-care.98  
* **Instead of** a public leaderboard, the app will use a **private, safety-moderated peer-support forum**.33

### **6.4 Final Recommendation**

The ideal product is a *student-focused hybrid* that integrates the market's best, evidence-based features. It will have the **clinical rigor** of 'Calm Health' (CBT, ACT, DBT) 79, the **AI-companion potential** of 'Wysa' 72, the **student-safe peer support** of 'TalkCampus' 45, the **acute-intervention brilliance** of 'Rootd' 34, and an **ethical engagement loop** that learns from Duolingo's *power* 91 but rejects its *punishing* mechanics.109 This is the blueprint for a product that can genuinely address the student mental health crisis.
