**Working-age Population**: 15 and older

**Labour Force**: all the people who are working or *actively* looking for work. 
- tldr anyone who could work on short notice
- Statistics Canada calculates the size of the labour force by *adding up all the people who were classified as employed and all the people who were classified as unemployed*.
- **Employed**: Anyone who did paid work, unpaid work for a family business, or worked for themselves
- **Unemployed**: People who don’t have a job but are willing and able to work and have looked for work in the last four weeks
- **Not in the labour force**: People who were unable or unwilling to do paid work
![[Pasted image 20250928140220.png]]


**Unemployment Rate**: the percentage of the labour force that is unemployed
- can affect things like elections
$$\text{Unemployment Rate} =\frac{\text{Number of unemployed}}{\text{Labour Force}}\cdot{100\%}$$

**Labour force *participation rate***:
$$\text{Participation Rate} = \frac{\text{Labour Force}}{\text{Working-age population}}\cdot{100\%}$$
**Unemployment-population ratio**: measures the portion of the population engaged in paid work
$$\text{Employment} - \text{population ratio} = \frac{\text{Number of employed}}{\text{Working-age population}}\cdot{100\%}$$
## Issues with the stats
**Discouraged Workers**: People who are available for work but have not looked for a job during the previous four weeks because they believe no jobs are available for them. *they are counted as "not in the labour force"*

Everyone with a job is considered employed even if its 1 hour a week.

## Trends in Labour Force Participation
The higher the labour force participation rate, the more labour is available and the higher a country’s levels of GDP and GDP per person

## How long are people unemployed
In an economic boom, the length of time a person is unemployed tends to be quite short. In a boom, many new firms are started and existing firms expand, increasing the opportunities for people to find work.

> [!error]
> **Changes in the overall unemployment rate don’t show you the whole picture of what is happening in the economy.** Even when the unemployment rate is rising, some firms will be growing, creating new jobs, and hiring new employees. # Types of Unemployment
- Frictional unemployment
- Structural unemployment
- Cyclical unemployment
- Seasonal unemployment

## Frictional Unemployment and Job Search
Frictional unemployment happens when workers are be**tween jobs or searching for new ones.** 
- It’s short-term and unavoidable because people need time to find jobs that match their skills and preferences, and firms also need time to find the right employees.
- Eliminating it wouldn’t be good for the economy, since job search helps workers find positions where they’re more satisfied and productive, which also benefits firms.

## Structural Unemployment
Structural unemployment happens when **workers’ skills don’t match the requirements of available jobs.**  *It often lasts longer because people need time to retrain or gain new skills.*
- Structural unemployment can also come from 
	- geographic mismatches (jobs in one city but workers in another)
	- legal changes (like the cod fishery closure in Newfoundland and Labrador)
	- workers lacking basic skills or having conditions that limit their ability to work.

## Cyclical Unemployment
Cyclical unemployment happens when a **recession** is happening in the business cycle 
Many firms find their sales falling and cut back on production. As production falls, **firms start laying off workers**.

## Season Unemployment
Seasonal unemployment happens when **jobs are only available at certain times of the year.**
- This makes *unemployment rates rise and fall at different times of the year.* 
	- To account for this, Statistics Canada reports both raw and seasonally adjusted unemployment rates, with the adjusted data giving a clearer picture of the overall labour market.

## Full Employment
**When the only remaining unemployment is structural and frictional unemployment**
Unemployment rate will most likely never be 0. Canada has never been under 5.6% in 42 years

**Natural rate of unemployment**: the sum of frictional and structural unemployment
- Fluctuations are mainly due to cyclical unemployment


> [!error]
>**The natural rate is a theoretical concept that represents the portion of the labour force that would be unemployed if everything in the economy was going well and only structural and frictional unemployment occur.** The official rate of unemployment reported by Statistics Canada is a measure of what is actually happening in the economy, and includes cyclical unemployment in addition to structural and frictional unemployment.

# Government Help
Governments can help reduce the level of frictional unemployment by pursuing policies that speed up the process of matching employees with employers. Governments can reduce structural unemployment by implementing policies that aid worker retraining.

Some government policies, however, can add to the level of frictional and structural unemployment. These government policies increase the unemployment rate either by increasing the time workers devote to searching for jobs, by providing disincentives for firms to hire workers, or by keeping wages above the market-clearing wage (or _equilibrium wage_).

**Employment Insurance (EI)**: is a government program that supports workers who lose their jobs. It replaces part of their income—about 55% up to a set maximum—so they can afford to spend more time searching for better jobs rather than immediately taking low-wage work. 
- *This extra time raises the unemployment rate but leads to better matches between workers and jobs,* making the economy more productive. EI also helps people avoid severe income loss, maintains consumer spending, and softens the impact of recessions. Most economists see it as beneficial overall.

## Minimum Wage
Every province and territory sets their own minimum wage
If the minimum wage is higher than the market-clearing wage, more people want to work than there are jobs available, creating unemployment. Teenagers, who often have fewer skills, are the group most affected

# Labour Unions
